### Slice-1 specification in Lean

Start `ConcreteSpec`, the specification of the new compiler, with slice 1 in one main file,
`ConcreteSpec/Spec.lean`. It is a provisional model, not yet normative: Core's shape is the owner's
A2 decision, and the file lists the choices that decision may change (string names, implicit
move/copy on read, typed literals, the body shape), the rule it does not model because its
criterion is open (ARITH-13), and the obligations it does not discharge (elaboration, lexer,
model translation, native image, erasure). It covers the eight integer types and `bool`, typed
`let`, `let _` of a Copy value, struct literals, struct-destructuring `let`, the move of a linear value and the use-after-move refusal,
consumption before `return`, used bindings, checked integer `+` with its overflow trap, and
`main` returning `u8`. Programs are abstract syntax; parsing and elaboration come later.

The typing (`HasType`), ownership (`Uses`), statement and body judgments are inductive relations,
independent of the executable checker. `check_iff` proves the checker agrees with them. The
semantics is a total, fuel-indexed evaluator whose outcomes separate exit, trap, stuck and
running out of fuel, with a fuel-free relation `Exec`. Proved, kernel only and without `sorry`:
fuel monotonicity, adequacy of the evaluator for `Exec`, determinism and totality of `Exec`,
that an accepted program never gets stuck and in particular never reads a moved variable, and
that it exits or traps. Each rule cites the decided rule it encodes, and conformance examples
(the move-then-consume program, use after move, an unconsumed value, `Copy` structs, unused
bindings, destructuring, wildcards, `let _` of Copy and linear values, overflow, literal range, mixed operand types, rebinding,
namespace collisions, the `u8` result) are checked by the kernel.

`ConcreteSpec` is a default Lake target and part of the external checker's population; the
pre-push hook routes a change to it to the external re-check job.
