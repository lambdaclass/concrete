### F16: scoped ownership protocols and loan histories

Add O3 Laws for downward reborrowing, linear move/reinitialization, loan retirement,
and deferred-call ownership, with populated Instances and one-rule Planted controls.
Reuse the actual ownership transitions and Exec dispatcher; do not introduce a
second executable ownership model or driver. O1/O2 retain their per-operation
preservation and erasure results.

The downward Law covers both modes, parent-before-child lifetime, child access,
conflicting parent suspension, upward refusal and exact restoration. The move Law
covers access refusal, second-consumption refusal and replacement only after
consumption. Retirement Laws cover finite LoanAction histories, place binding and
storage teardown, and fresh direct-call argument issuance. The unified finite-history Law also
covers view arguments, standalone downward entry, and every lifecycle event.

Deferred lifetime is conditional on explicit certificates of actual machine steps
and admitted ownership effects. It quantifies over arbitrary finite callee prefixes;
it is not a checker implementation or a theorem that unchecked bodies are admitted.
Separate Laws cover registration without argument evaluation, usable adoption in
both modes with same-root parents/ancestors, return-boundary release and caller-loan
retention, restored caller-parent access, and storage-only scope cleanup. Nested
calls use the substituted lifecycle callback; shared loans freeze owner writes,
and mutable loans also freeze owner reads.
Concrete executions use real late argument evaluation and run the enclosing scope
to normal completion; they are Instances, not the universal Law.
