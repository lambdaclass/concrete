Add the closed integer/bool constant initializer specification and its rule-registry
entry. Initializers use the shared Scalar carriers and primitive semantics, with no
calls, loops or effects; every initializer, including unused declarations, must
succeed. Arithmetic failure and fuel exhaustion are distinct refusals. Source
forward references are dependency-sorted; cycles carry their actual chain.

Language authority on main is `docs/decisions.md#decided-2026-11-14-constant-types`
(closed integer/bool admission and checked arithmetic, with overflow refused),
`#recovered-29-18` and `#decided-2026-11-08-constants` (closed grammar, mandatory
evaluation and refusal on failure or exhausted fuel), `#recovered-29-19` (forward
references and cycle chains), and `#recovered-59-26` (compile-time Copy values).
`#od106` makes try_convert Result-valued, outside this grammar. Under
`#decided-2026-11-13-proof-labels`, the constant examples and registry checks are
Instance checks, not Law evidence; general constant laws belong to F07.

Rule evidence requires closed, inspectable data after default-transparency
reduction. One depth-bounded total traversal checks every constructor argument,
including parameters and dictionaries, recursively inspects type expressions and
indices, and refuses opaque or stuck data, function data, sort-valued runtime
fields, open terms and reduction errors. Proof internals are left to kernel
checking and the axiom inventory/trust ban: an opaque dependency inside a proof
does not make the adjacent data unknown. Reduction may erase unused arguments
and annotations; concreteness concerns the resulting data, not source provenance.

The registry takes monomorphic ScalarConstDecl data. ConstsPermitted embeds each
declaration field for field into the existing generic ConstsAdmitted judgment,
preserving constant semantics without exposing a semantic-carrier dictionary as
runtime example data or exempting its fields from inspection. Its registry
Instances accept a dependency example and refuse an unused overflow.

Controls cover arithmetic endpoints and failures, admission independently of typing
and evaluation, dependency ordering, cycle diagnostics and distinct fuel refusal.
Registry controls refuse opaque parameters and indices, proposition inputs,
parameterized families and non-concrete runtime fields, including nested data.
Positive controls retain ModuleId, opaque-core proof fields and data made concrete
by reduction; they require the intended diagnostic without an invalid-example
diagnostic. Proof-only synthetic carriers explicitly inhabit Type.
