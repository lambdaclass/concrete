The owner asked for a side-by-side comparison of two capability designs for Concrete:
- **TODAY (decided):** compile-time `with(...)` permission sets on functions, checked up to main and erased, PLUS runtime grant values handed to main (linear, narrowable, consumed on close). Allocators are two-tier: authority via the capability, identity via an explicit allocator value. Callbacks declare capabilities at the use site.
- **MERGED (proposed, undecided):** capabilities are values only, the object-capability model. There is no `with(...)`. A function's authority is exactly the capability values it receives (borrowed for use, moved to give away or close). Pure permissions are zero-sized unforgeable tokens. main receives the startup grants as parameters. Narrowing derives weaker values. One allocator value carries both authority and identity. Callbacks carry capabilities in their explicit context. Long-lived structs own their capabilities, because references are second-class. Authority facts are derived and reported by the compiler.
