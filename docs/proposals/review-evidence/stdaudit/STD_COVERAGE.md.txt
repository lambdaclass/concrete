# Std coverage audit against Rust, Zig, Go and C/POSIX (PROPOSED, draft 3, 2026-11-08)

Status: PROPOSED. An audit; it decides nothing. It answers the owner's question "isn't many things
of stdlib missing?" by comparing the current std plan, category by category, with the standard
libraries of Rust (core/alloc/std), Zig, Go and C/POSIX (current stable releases as of 2026-11; for
C, C23 and POSIX.1-2024; library extensions and external packages are marked as such). Language
questions are listed for the owner in section 6 and are not decided here. Drafts 2 and 3 apply the
round-1 and round-2 reviews of Codex, Kimi and agy (section 7).

Read against (branch `wip/record-language` at `1547734f`): `ROADMAP.md` ("Vertical slices",
"Showcases", "Std per slice"); `docs/PHILOSOPHY.md`; `docs/decisions.md` (Core rows Q1–Q9, OD13,
OD51, OD136, OD148, the 2026-11-02 filesystem rule, the 2026-11-06 no-hidden-traps rule, the
2026-11-08 rows); `docs/proposals/EXAMPLES.md`; the hosted grant table approved as Q7.10 (R4 F6 in
`legacy/docs/archive/decisions-2026-11-05/R4_RATIONALE.md`); `legacy/docs/spec/` (OWN-9, REF-5,
TRUST-7, ARITH-1/6/11/12); `legacy/std/src/*.con`; and the scratch proposals `STD_PLACEMENT.md`
(draft 3) and `OWNER_QUESTIONS.md` (O1–O12), with `LANGUAGE_DECISIONS_2026-11-08.md`.

## 1. The short answer

Yes: measured by module count, the plan carries a small fraction of what Rust, Zig or Go ship. Most
of that is on purpose. Rule 17 and the placement rule P1 admit a module only when an adopted
showcase stage consumes it, and a few features are refused outright: closures, collection loop
syntax for now (OD51), variadic functions, macros and derived formatting, implicit conversions,
and concurrency before slice 28.

Module count is the wrong measure of risk. The audit finds three kinds of real issue:

1. **A small number of definite gaps where an adopted stage needs something the plan does not place
   or schedule** (section 4.1). The most important: OD13 decided that a value may move out "through
   an explicit replace or take through `&mut T`", and Q3.1's approved reasoning keeps "general
   mutable `replace`/`swap`" on views, but no slice schedules either in safe code. The slice table
   lists only `swap_at` at 15, which needs a substitute value in hand. `OrderedMap` at 23, as
   proposed (ordinary safe std code over a balanced tree, before `trusted fn` at 24), rotates `Heap`
   nodes behind `&mut` and needs the place-level form. Agy points out that the
   heap move-out of item 12 cannot replace it: it consumes the `Heap` and releases its storage, so a
   rotation would have to re-allocate fallibly mid-operation. Legacy std never tested this: its
   `OrderedMap`, `Vec`, deque and binary heap were all `trusted` raw-pointer code
   (`legacy/std/src/ordered_map.con` is a sorted array behind `*mut K`). Other definite gaps: line
   framing over partial reads (grep 16), the unscheduled operation registry for already-decided
   filesystem operations, the float operations Wasm 26 needs beyond ARITH-1/6/11/12, and the
   large-file strategy of the file encryptor at zero heap.
2. **Conditional needs** (section 4.2): items an adopted stage needs only if its implementation
   or profile chooses a particular design (a bitset, byte-string keys, `pread`, buffered output, an
   in-buffer `memmove`). These are
   showcase design choices, not gaps, until the showcase chooses.
3. **Design questions no adopted showcase exercises**: sorting and heaps of linear elements, graphs
   with parent links, borrowing traversal with early exit, one stream interface across capabilities.
   Section 5 proposes small probes. Compiled probes are the validation (rule 17). Paper sketches
   before the Core freeze (A2c, before slice 1) only find questions; they validate nothing.

## 2. Classification

- **(a) covered**: placed in the plan (slice given), or showcase-local under P2 (the showcase's own
  subject stays in it).
- **(b) deliberately out**: a decided row or a recorded refusal excludes exactly that feature
  (cited).
- **(c) real gap**: an adopted showcase stage needs it and the plan does not place or schedule it
  (stage named). **(c?)** marks a conditional need that depends on a showcase design choice.
- **(d) design validation**: no showcase needs it; either a probe is proposed (section 5) or a reason
  is given why one is not worth it.

Risk tags follow `STD_PLACEMENT.md`: **R-ref** second-class references at scale, **R-lin** linear
ownership in containers, **R-trap** the proof cost of no hidden traps, **R-err** explicit error
propagation, **R-cb** callbacks with contracts, **R-grant** grants and `with(...)`, **R-ct**
constant time and secrets.

## 4. Gaps

### 4.1 Definite gaps: an adopted stage needs it, and nothing places or schedules it

- **G1. Displacing a linear value behind `&mut` in safe code (owner, S1). Stage: `OrderedMap` at
  23 as proposed, consumed by KV 23, EVM 23 and Wasm 23.** The operation itself is decided, in two
  places:
  - OD13 (2026-10-29): "a value moves out of raw memory only through `read_owned(p)` or an explicit
    replace or take through `&mut T`". TRUST-7's accepted example writes that replace in `trusted`
    code with `read_owned`;
  - Q3.1's approved reasoning keeps "general mutable `replace`/`swap`" on views.

  What is missing is a schedule and a safe signature. The slice table lists only `swap_at(v, i, x)`
  at 15 (Q3.6), which needs `x` in hand. No slice schedules a safe place-level replace or take for
  struct fields and heap-borrowed places before `trusted fn` arrives at 24. OWN-9 refuses
  overwriting a live linear value. Item 12's heap move-out consumes a `Heap` and releases its
  storage, so a rotation built on it would re-allocate fallibly in the middle of `insert` or
  `remove`. For an `Option` slot, `None` is a ready substitute, so a place-level replace also covers
  take.

  The need follows from the representation `STD_PLACEMENT.md` proposes: a balanced tree of `Heap`
  nodes, with a logarithmic step bound among its laws. A sorted-array map over `Vec` (the legacy
  design) inserts and removes using `push`, `pop` and `swap_at` with the displaced entry as the
  substitute, and needs no place-level replace, but its insertion is linear rather than
  logarithmic. S1 lists both.
- **G2. Line framing over partial reads (std `io`, a module from 17; code inside grep at 16).
  Stages: grep 16 (stdin), grep 18 (file); likely also KV 23 (log replay) and server 27 (request
  head).** `read` may return any prefix, and lines cross read boundaries. The second-class shape is a
  `Copy`-coordinate scanner over a caller-owned buffer: `fill(scan, buf, stream)`, then
  `next_line(scan, buf) -> Option<Range>`, with the caller forming the subview by the one borrow
  form (P4). The showcase must also state what it does with a line longer than its buffer (flag F1).
- **G3. Operation contracts for decided filesystem operations (owner: an operation registry, not
  policy).** The 2026-11-02 filesystem rule already authorizes "opening, creating, listing, probing,
  removing, renaming, linking and metadata lookup", each with a `Directory` and `with(Fs)`. R4 F6 is
  an incomplete registry: it gives the rights and the `Directory`, `File` and grant lifecycles, but no
  signatures for those operations (`STD_PLACEMENT.md` sketches only `open`, `read`, `write` and
  `close`). Before slice 18 closes, the registry needs exact contracts for the operations a stage
  uses: `open` with its modes, `read` and `write`; metadata lookup if the server sends
  `Content-Length` (chunked or close-delimited responses are the alternatives, RFC 9112 §6.3); and
  `rename`/`remove` if the KV store compacts. Only genuinely new semantics need fresh owner
  treatment: append mode, positional I/O and socket read timeouts (flag F3).
- **G4. Bit operations on public integers (std placement; Q9.6). Stages: Keccak 21 (64-bit lane
  rotations), Wasm 21 (`clz`, `ctz`, `popcnt`, `rotl`, `rotr`); later SHA-256 and BLAKE2s.** ChaCha20
  rotates `Secret<u32>` words by public counts through the slice-7 constant-time table (the
  information-flow row; `OWNER_QUESTIONS.md` O7 marks this decided), so it is not a consumer of the
  public form. All of these are reducible, so Q9.6 already answers that they are ordinary std code: a
  small `bits` module, code at 21. A primitive (an LLVM intrinsic) would be a recorded revision of
  Q9.6 with measured need.
- **G5. Float operations beyond ARITH-1/6/11/12 (owner, S5). Stage: Wasm 26.**
  - Already covered: rounding and no contraction, comparisons, the conversions, the float-to-integer
    roundings that return `try_convert` errors, and remainder.
  - Missing, for every Wasm profile: `sqrt`; `ceil`, `floor`, `trunc` and `nearest` returning floats
    (distinct from ARITH-11's float-to-integer operations, and defined on infinities, NaNs and signed
    zeros); `min` and `max` with Wasm's NaN and signed-zero rules; `copysign`, `abs` and `neg` as
    sign-bit operations; bit reinterpretation.
  - Missing for some profiles: the saturating `trunc_sat` conversions, only if the Wasm profile
    includes them (flag F8).
  - An observation policy. Wasm lets an arithmetic NaN result have a nondeterministic sign and, in
    the relevant cases, a nondeterministic payload, while `abs`, `neg` and `copysign` are
    deterministic on bits. Reinterpretation and stores can expose those bits to later output, so
    "exclude NaNs from the comparison" is not sound.

  Wasm 21 also needs `rem_s` to return 0 for `MIN % -1`, and `div_s` there to be a Wasm trap. The
  interpreter handles both explicitly with `checked_*` (12): no gap, but a conformance item for the
  showcase.
- **G6. Large files at zero heap (showcase claim, owner). Stage: file encryptor at 18.** The sketched
  `aead::seal/open` and `poly1305::mac` are one-shot over in-memory buffers, and slice 18 has no heap.
  For files larger than the fixed buffer:
  - **two passes** (incremental Poly1305 to verify, then decrypt) keep "no plaintext before the tag
    verifies" only under a premise that the file does not change between passes. Nothing decided
    guarantees that, and zero heap cannot retain the authenticated bytes;
  - **a chunked construction** (each chunk sealed under a counter nonce, with a last-chunk flag
    against truncation and reordering) changes the release law from per-file to per-chunk;
  - **a stated maximum file size** that fits the buffer.

### 4.2 Conditional needs: showcase design or profile choices

These become gaps only when the showcase picks the design or profile, and are placed then (P1; P2
puts a substrate with one adopted consumer in std).

- **K1. Bitset.** grep 16's NFA state sets and EVM 15's JUMPDEST validation may use bit sets.
  JUMPDEST validation is mandatory; a bitmap is one representation. At zero heap the size matters:
  the EIP-170 code limit of 24,576 bytes is 3 KiB as bits, or 24 KiB as `[bool; N]`, beside a 32 KiB
  operand stack, under the stack budget.
- **K2. Lexicographic byte comparison.** Needed if KV 23 keys are byte strings: `bytes::cmp`, and
  `Ord` impls for `Vec<u8, A>` and `String<A>` (Q4.3 fixes lexicographic byte order).
- **K3. Positional read and append mode.** Needed if KV 23's index maps keys to log offsets, or if the
  log is reopened for appending. Replaying to the end of the file can also establish the write
  position.
- **K4. HTTP helpers.** `ascii::eq_ignore_case` (case-insensitive header names) and a multi-byte
  `bytes::find_seq` (CRLF CRLF), at 27, if the server or httpget code uses them; hex chunk-size
  parsing for httpget (section 3).
- **K5. Buffered output.** The speed target is set from grep's slice-16 measurements (R-0623) and is
  report-only until then. Measure first; a showcase-local buffer is the first experiment, and a std
  writer follows only if a target or the measurements need one. Its shape is flag F0.
- **K6. Copy inside one buffer.** `bytes::copy(dst, src)` cannot copy within one buffer, because a
  mutable and a shared view of one root conflict (Q3.4). Wasm's `memory.copy` (bulk memory, flag F8)
  and the EVM's `MCOPY` (flag F5) need `bytes::copy_within(v, src, dst, len)` with the `memmove` law,
  if their profiles include them. DEFLATE (PNG 21–22) needs a different contract: a forward,
  byte-by-byte copy whose overlap replicates recent bytes (distance 1, length 5 repeats one byte).
  It is a loop inside the showcase (P2), which needs no std change.

## 5. Design probes (category d)

A probe is **not a std module**. Its declared consumer is design validation, and its output is an
answer or a sharper owner question. Compiled probes at their slice are the validation (rule 17). A
paper sketch written before the Core freeze (A2c, before slice 1) is a question-finding exercise:
it can show that a decided construct is missing, and nothing more. The case for doing the sketches
early is that a missing Core operation found at slice 23 would reopen a frozen row.

|---|---|---|---|

Not worth a probe:

- **N1. JSON or another recursive format.** The risks are parsing untrusted nesting (stack bounds,
  termination) and destroying a deep owned tree. grep 22's regex parser (nested groups) and Wasm 21's
  nested blocks cover the first; `OrderedMap`'s `Destroy` at 23 covers bounded-depth recursive
  destruction. If grep 22 does not build its syntax tree from `Heap` nodes, add iterative
  destruction of a deep owned chain to D1b.
- **N2. Calendars.** No consumer. The one possible use is an HTTP `Date` header from the server at
  27, which needs only a days-to-civil-date conversion inside the showcase (flag F3).
- **N3. A deque as a module.** A safe deque fits `Vec<Option<T>>` or `HeapArray<Option<T>>`: one tag
  per slot, which with today's `i32` enum tags (foundation question 4) is a large overhead for small
  `T`. That is a representation and performance tradeoff to answer with O3 and niches (item 58), not a
  language gap.
- **N4. Float parsing and formatting.** No consumer: Wasm uses binary encodings, and the slice-26
  example is only *proposed*. Shortest round-trip formatting is a large algorithm with a large proof;
  it would test proof cost (R-trap), but at a size that needs a consumer (flag F6).
- **The rest.** Linked lists, tries, permutations, fixed point, general bignum, UTF-16, Unicode
  tables, seeded PRNGs, compression encoders, TLS, UDP, logging, sets and parser combinators. No
  consumer, and no risk that a probe above or an adopted stage does not already test.

## 6. Questions for the owner

Language questions, with options and a recommendation each; none is decided here.

- **S1. Scheduling the decided displacement operations in safe code** (OD13; Q3.1's reasoning;
  Q3.6). Options:
  - (a) a safe place-level `replace(p: &mut T, x: T) -> T` at slice 13, the safe form of OD13's
    "explicit replace or take through `&mut T`" (it covers `take` of an `Option` slot with `None`);
    plus the two-index `swap(v: ViewMut<T>, i, j)` of Q3.1's reasoning added to the slice-15 view
    operations, for permutations that have no substitute in hand (reverse, rotate, an arbitrary
    permutation; insertion sort and heap sift can use `swap_at` with the moving element);
  - (b) `replace` at 13 only, with view permutations done by `swap_at` or by `drain`/`map_into`
    rebuilds;
  - (c) neither before 24, with `OrderedMap` as a sorted array over `Vec` (linear insertion, which
    revises the logarithmic step-bound law `STD_PLACEMENT.md` proposes), or as a tree with by-value
    APIs that thread the map (`insert(m: OrderedMap<…>, …) -> Inserted<…>`), whose rotations still
    need OD13's operation or a fallible re-allocation.

  Recommendation: (a). It schedules operations the record already decided or reasoned, and both forms
  return every displaced value explicitly. Adding them to slices 13 and 15 extends Q3.6's slice-15
  list, so it is a recorded Core revision before A2c. Exact names belong to the spelling round.
- **S2. Shared references to two elements of one view** (extends O4). May a borrow over a view take
  shared references to two of its elements at once, for `Ord::cmp`? Q3.1's "no element references" is
  about heap-array opening; REF-5 allows index projection through a reference. Tree nodes are not
  affected (field projection through heap borrows). Options: (a) yes, shared element references from
  a view inside a borrow block, never returned or stored; (b) no, comparison by a callback receiving
  the two elements (20). Recommendation: (a), consistent with REF-5 and needing no function value.
- **S3. One stream interface.** The existing forms suffice for one law. Options: (a) per-family
  functions (`console`, `fs`, `net`), with the partial-write law stated once in Lean over an abstract
  writer; (b) one generic function with an explicit capability parameter, taking the write operation
  as a function value (20); (c) an associated-capability trait (new language; OD148 refuses trait type
  parameters). Recommendation: (a) at 18 and (b) at 20, compared in D4; (c) only if both fail.
- **S4. A borrowing traversal callback type** (extends O4 and Q2.4). Termination is already decided
  (evidence, or a written permission at the indirect call; 2026-11-06). Open: the callback type for
  non-consuming traversal and its stop value. Options: (a) reuse `Step` with a borrowing callback;
  (b) a coordinate cursor only (no new callback type). Recommendation: decide after D3 compares them.
- **S5. Float operations and NaN observations at 26** (G5). Options for NaNs: (a) a canonical NaN
  for arithmetic results in the Lean semantics, with the backends made to match and sign-bit
  operations exact; (b) Wasm-style allowed-result sets, with a relational comparison of observations
  that follows bits through reinterpretation and memory; (c) bit-exact backend payloads as a named
  premise. For the operations: which are primitives (`sqrt` and the float-result roundings are
  correctly rounded IEEE operations; `min`/`max` have incompatible NaN and signed-zero definitions).
  Recommendation: (a), the most transparent. The Wasm interpreter then always produces the canonical
  NaN, a member of every set Wasm allows.

Not language questions, flagged for the owner:

- **F0.** The shape of a buffered reader or writer: owning its stream by value (returned with any
  error by `finish`) or taking the stream on every call. Both are expressible; rule 2 needs one.
- **F1.** grep's behaviour on a line longer than its fixed buffer at 16 and 18 (refuse with a typed
  error, or match a prefix), stated in its claim.
- **F2.** The file encryptor's large-file strategy (G6), with the already-flagged nonce scheme.
- **F3.** New grant-table semantics (G3): append mode, positional I/O, socket read timeouts. Without
  timeouts, one idle client stops the sequential server at 27, a liveness limit its claims should
  state. Also whether the server sends an HTTP `Date` header (note N2).
- **F4.** Whether the KV store at 23 supports ordered scans or compaction (traversal, S4; `rename`).
- **F5.** The EVM fork, that is, its opcode set (`MCOPY`, transient storage, `CLZ`), for K6 and G4.
- **F6.** The slice-26 *proposed* mean/variance example: no float formatting is planned (note N4),
  so it should report bits or integer parts.
- **F7.** Std placement of the conditional items K1–K6 when their showcase chooses them. Also the
  `u64_hex` entries of `STD_PLACEMENT.md`'s `parse` and `fmt`: parsing has a conditional consumer
  (httpget's chunked responses); formatting has none.
- **F8.** The Wasm profile: version and extensions (bulk memory, saturating conversions, sign
  extension, SIMD, the deterministic profile), for K6, G4 and G5.
